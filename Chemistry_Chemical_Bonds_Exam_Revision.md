## 1. What is a Chemical Bond?

A **chemical bond** is the force of attraction that holds atoms or ions together in a molecule or compound.

### Main types covered

1. **Covalent bond**
2. **Ionic bond**
3. **Hydrogen bond**
4. **Co-ordinate bond**

---

# 2. Covalent Bond

## Definition

A **covalent bond** is a chemical bond formed by the **mutual sharing of one or more pairs of electrons** between two atoms.

### Example: Formation of H₂

Each hydrogen atom has **1 electron**. The two hydrogen atoms share their electrons so that each gets a stable duplet.

### Diagram

```text
Before sharing:

H •              • H
1 electron       1 electron


After sharing:

H : H

or

H — H

       ↑
   Shared pair
   of electrons
```

Therefore, H₂ contains a **single covalent bond**.

### Remember

> **Covalent = Sharing of electrons**

---

# 3. Ionic Bond

## Definition

An **ionic bond** is a chemical bond formed by the **complete transfer of one or more electrons** from one atom to another, followed by electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.

### Example: Formation of NaCl

Sodium has one valence electron and chlorine needs one electron to complete its outer shell.

### Step 1 — Sodium loses an electron

```text
Na  →  Na⁺ + e⁻
```

### Step 2 — Chlorine gains the electron

```text
Cl + e⁻  →  Cl⁻
```

### Step 3 — Oppositely charged ions attract

```text
Na⁺  +  Cl⁻  →  NaCl
        ↑
 Electrostatic
  attraction
```

### Diagrammatic idea

```text
Na                 Cl
 |                  |
loses 1 e⁻       gains 1 e⁻
 ↓                  ↑
Na⁺      e⁻        Cl⁻

        ↓

       Na⁺ ··· Cl⁻
          attraction
```

Therefore, NaCl is an **ionic compound**.

### Remember

> **Ionic = Transfer of electrons**

---

# 4. Hydrogen Bond

## Definition

A **hydrogen bond** is a weak attractive force between a hydrogen atom attached to a highly electronegative atom such as **fluorine (F), oxygen (O), or nitrogen (N)** and another electronegative atom.

### Example: Hydrogen bonding in H₂O

Water molecules attract one another because the hydrogen of one water molecule is attracted to the oxygen of another water molecule.

### Diagram

```text
   H                  H
   |                  |
H — O  ···········  H — O
       ↑
  Hydrogen bond
```

A clearer representation is:

```text
H — O — H  ···  O — H
             ↑
      Hydrogen bond
```

`···` represents the hydrogen bond.

### Important condition

Hydrogen bonding commonly occurs when:

```text
H — F
H — O
H — N
```

### Remember

> **Hydrogen bond = H attached to F, O or N + attraction**

### Important exam point

A hydrogen bond is generally **weaker than a normal covalent or ionic bond**.

---

# 5. Co-ordinate Bond

## Definition

A **co-ordinate bond** is a covalent bond in which **both electrons of the shared pair are donated by the same atom**.

It is also called a **dative bond**.

### Example: Formation of NH₄⁺

Ammonia (NH₃) contains a **lone pair of electrons on nitrogen**.

A hydrogen ion (H⁺) has no electron available for sharing.

Nitrogen donates its lone pair to H⁺.

### Diagram

```text
        H
        |
H — N :  +  H⁺
        |
        H

        ↓

        H
        |
H — N → H
        |
        H

       NH₄⁺
```

The arrow `→` shows that **both bonding electrons are donated by nitrogen**.

### Reaction

```text
NH₃ + H⁺  →  NH₄⁺
```

### Remember

> **Co-ordinate = One atom donates both electrons**

# 6. Most Important Difference

| Bond | Main process | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Covalent** | Sharing of electrons | H₂ |
| **Ionic** | Transfer of electrons | NaCl |
| **Hydrogen** | Attraction involving H and F/O/N | H₂O |
| **Co-ordinate** | Both shared electrons donated by one atom | NH₄⁺ |

# 7. Covalent vs Ionic vs Co-ordinate

### Covalent Bond

```text
Atom A  •  +  •  Atom B
             ↓
        Shared electrons
```

**Both atoms contribute electrons.**

---

### Ionic Bond

```text
Atom A  ── e⁻ ──→  Atom B
   ↓                  ↓
  A⁺                 B⁻

        A⁺ ←──→ B⁻
```

**One atom transfers electrons to another.**

---

### Co-ordinate Bond

```text
Atom A :  +  Atom B
   ↓
Both electrons donated
by Atom A

Atom A → Atom B
```

**One atom contributes both electrons of the shared pair.**

# 8. One-Line Definitions for Exams

### Covalent Bond
A covalent bond is a bond formed by the **mutual sharing of one or more pairs of electrons** between two atoms.

### Ionic Bond
An ionic bond is a bond formed by the **complete transfer of one or more electrons** from one atom to another and electrostatic attraction between the resulting ions.

### Hydrogen Bond
A hydrogen bond is a **weak attractive force** between hydrogen attached to F, O or N and another electronegative atom.

### Co-ordinate Bond
A co-ordinate bond is a covalent bond in which **both shared electrons are donated by the same atom**.

> **C — Share**  
> **I — Transfer**

> **C — One atom gives both**

```text

```

# 10. Common Exam Mistakes

### ❌ Mistake 1
Saying that ionic bonds are formed by sharing electrons.

### ✅ Correct
Ionic bonds are formed by **transfer of electrons**.

---

### ❌ Mistake 2
Saying that a co-ordinate bond has no sharing.

### ✅ Correct
It **is a covalent bond**, but **both electrons in the shared pair come from the same atom**.

---

### ❌ Mistake 3
Calling hydrogen bonding a normal covalent bond.

### ✅ Correct
Hydrogen bonding is an **attractive intermolecular force** (in the usual school-level treatment).

---

### ❌ Mistake 4
Forgetting the atoms involved in hydrogen bonding.

### ✅ Correct

```text
F — H
O — H
N — H
```

Hydrogen bonding is especially associated with **F, O and N**.

# 11. Exam-Ready Answer

## Q. Explain covalent, ionic, hydrogen and co-ordinate bonds with one example each.

### Answer:

**1. Covalent Bond:**  
A covalent bond is a chemical bond formed by the mutual sharing of one or more pairs of electrons between two atoms.  
**Example:** H₂

```text
H — H
```

**2. Ionic Bond:**  
An ionic bond is a chemical bond formed by the complete transfer of one or more electrons from one atom to another, followed by electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions.  
**Example:** NaCl

```text
Na → Na⁺ + e⁻
Cl + e⁻ → Cl⁻
Na⁺ + Cl⁻ → NaCl
```

**3. Hydrogen Bond:**  
A hydrogen bond is a weak attractive force between hydrogen attached to a highly electronegative atom such as F, O or N and another electronegative atom.  
**Example:** H₂O

```text
H — O — H  ···  O — H
             ↑
       Hydrogen bond
```

**4. Co-ordinate Bond:**  
A co-ordinate bond is a covalent bond in which both electrons of the shared pair are donated by the same atom. It is also called a dative bond.  
**Example:** NH₄⁺

```text
NH₃ + H⁺ → NH₄⁺
      N → H
```
